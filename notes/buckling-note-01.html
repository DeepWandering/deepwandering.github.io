Periodic Buckling of Thin Films: Basic Energy Balance

Introduction

Periodic buckling is a common deformation mode when a thin film is compressed or coupled to a compliant environment. A useful first model is to compare stretching, bending, and substrate-related energy terms. The total energy can be written schematically as \(E_{\mathrm{tot}} = E_{\mathrm{stretch}} + E_{\mathrm{bend}} + E_{\mathrm{sub}}\).

Bending Energy

For a small out-of-plane displacement field \(h(x,y)\), a typical bending energy has the form

\[ E_{\mathrm{bend}} = \frac{\kappa}{2} \int \left( \nabla^2 h \right)^2 \, dA \]

where \(\kappa\) is the bending rigidity. This term penalizes rapid curvature variation and therefore competes with the release of compressive strain energy.

Energy Balance

In a minimal scaling picture, a sinusoidal profile can be represented by amplitude \(A\) and wavelength \(\lambda\). A simplified balance may be organized as

\[ \begin{aligned} E_{\mathrm{tot}}(A,\lambda) &= E_{\mathrm{stretch}}(A,\lambda) + E_{\mathrm{bend}}(A,\lambda) + E_{\mathrm{sub}}(A,\lambda) \\ &\approx C_s \varepsilon^2 A_0 + C_b \kappa \frac{A^2}{\lambda^4} A_0 + C_{\mathrm{sub}} K A^2 A_0 . \end{aligned} \]

The constants in this expression depend on the exact geometry and assumptions. The value of the expression is mainly conceptual: it separates the physical mechanisms that control pattern selection.
