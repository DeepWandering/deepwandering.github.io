Research

My work connects solid mechanics, computational modeling, and lattice descriptions of electronic and topological phenomena in low-dimensional materials.

Periodic Buckling of Two-Dimensional Materials

Thin films and two-dimensional materials under compression can form periodic buckling or wrinkling patterns. I am interested in how elastic energy, geometric constraints, and boundary conditions determine the wavelength, amplitude, and stability of these patterns.

This direction provides a mechanics-based route to understand controlled deformation in graphene and related materials.

Strain Engineering and Electronic Structure

Mechanical deformation can modify the local lattice geometry of graphene-based systems and thereby influence their electronic structure. The central question is how strain fields, especially periodic ones, couple to effective Hamiltonians and transport-relevant quantities.

I use continuum mechanics and numerical models to build interpretable links between deformation modes and electronic responses.

Topological Phase Transitions in Haldane-Type Models

Haldane-type lattice models are useful minimal systems for studying topology without relying on a net magnetic field. I focus on how model parameters and strain-induced modifications can drive changes in band topology.

Wilson loops and hybrid Wannier function centers are used as numerical diagnostics for tracking topological phase transitions.

Multiscale Simulation Methods

The mechanics of thin films and two-dimensional materials often requires connecting atomistic, continuum, and reduced-order descriptions. I use tools such as LAMMPS, ABAQUS, MATLAB, and Python to study deformation patterns across different levels of resolution.

The emphasis is on reproducible workflows that keep physical assumptions explicit and computational results interpretable.
